\title{From Endogenous Credit to Borrower Composition\\
\large A Reproducible Measure from Japanese Sectoral Loan Stocks}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Bank lending can grow while its borrower mix changes. Endogenous-credit theory
distinguishes credit creation from credit allocation, but public data often
show only total lending or borrower-sector stocks. We construct a reproducible
borrower-composition measure from Bank of Japan sectoral loan stocks. The
published Japan crosswalk of \citet{BezemerSamarinaZhang2020} divides one loan
population into non-financial business, financial business, property and
mortgages, and household non-housing credit. Stock changes are summed within
each bucket before positive parts are taken, so finer classifications within a
bucket do not alter the four-quarter vector. We also report alternative
placements of construction based on \citet{Werner1997} and
\citet{MullerVerner2024}. Accounting and coverage checks support the bridge;
external data provide only partial checks of selected branches. In the
current-vintage pseudo-out-of-sample application, five of six main estimates
have higher point loss than the matched credit-stock baseline. The remaining
main estimate and one Appendix estimate are lower, but both intervals include
zero. The result is a reproducible measure of borrower composition, not loan
purpose or a forecasting result.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} endogenous credit; borrower composition; sectoral
lending; measurement; Bank of Japan

\section{Introduction}

Bank lending creates deposits and cannot be reduced to a prior stock of
loanable funds \citep{LeBourva1992,Fontana2003,BoE2014}. Endogenous-money and
monetary-circuit theories also make the location of credit important. Loans to
non-financial firms, construction companies, financial intermediaries, and
households enter different balance-sheet positions even when total lending is
unchanged \citep{RochonRossi2007,Passarella2014,Werner2012}. Credit scale alone
does not capture this difference.

Empirical studies find different macro-financial patterns for mortgage and
business credit \citep{BezemerSamarinaZhang2020,BezemerZhang2019}, tradable and
non-tradable sector credit
\citep{MullerVerner2024,AndriesOngenaSprincean2025}, and riskier corporate
credit allocation \citep{BrandaoMarquesEtAl2022}. Research on Japan also shows
shifts and dispersion in sectoral loan growth \citep{SaitaSekine2001}. Yet a
basic measurement problem remains. The Bank of Japan (BOJ) reports detailed
loan stocks by borrower sector, but not comprehensive gross originations
classified jointly by borrower and purpose. Stock changes can reflect new
lending, repayment, write-offs, or reclassification. Borrower labels therefore
do not reveal what a loan financed.

This paper addresses the observable part of that problem. It constructs and
audits a borrower-composition measure based on the published Japan crosswalk of
\citet{BezemerSamarinaZhang2020}. The measure divides one included loan
population into non-financial business (NFB), financial business, property and
mortgages, and household non-housing credit. Credit scale is calculated from
the same population. The scalar \(q_t\) is the NFB coordinate of this
four-part vector. It is not a share of credit reaching a final destination.

Construction shows why the classification must remain visible. It belongs to
NFB in the primary Japan crosswalk, enters the financial-circulation proxy in
\citet{Werner1997}, and is non-tradable in \citet{MullerVerner2024}. We apply
all three classifications to the same loan population and report them
together. This turns a hidden classification choice into a direct robustness
check. The earlier author-defined G/B/E grouping remains only in the Appendix
for archive compatibility.

The BOJ mapping, coverage, residual accounting, and positive and net changes
form the main evidence. MOF and MLIT data provide partial checks for
manufacturing and household housing. A pseudo-out-of-sample comparison shows
how the measure can be used with a matched credit-stock baseline, although it
does not establish a forecast improvement in the present sample. The
contribution is an auditable measurement bridge.

\section{Conceptual Motivation and Measurement Design}

\subsection{From endogenous credit to two observable coordinates}

Classical quantity theories relate nominal expenditure to money and its
velocity \citep{Fisher1911,Friedman1956,Laidler1985}. Endogenous-credit accounts
instead start from bank lending and deposit creation
\citep{LeBourva1992,Fontana2003,BoE2014}. The Quantity Theory of Credit further
emphasises that the economic use of credit conditions its transmission
\citep{Werner2012}. Monetary-circuit theory supplies an institutional account:
bank credit opens and finances circuits whose balance-sheet positions need not
be equivalent \citep{RochonRossi2007,Passarella2014}.

The available BOJ data do not identify those uses. They do identify borrower
sectors and two household-purpose components. Let \(B_t^{TOT}\) be the BOJ
total for domestically licensed banks, where ``domestically licensed''
describes the lenders rather than borrower geography. Let \(B_t^{FIN}\),
\(B_t^{RE}\), \(B_t^{LG}\), \(B_t^{HH}\), and \(B_t^{HOU}\) denote,
respectively, finance and insurance, real estate, local-government, total
household, and household housing stocks. The primary Japan crosswalk defines
\begin{align}
 L_t^{NFB}
   &=B_t^{TOT}-B_t^{FIN}-B_t^{RE}-B_t^{LG}-B_t^{HH}, \\
 L_t^{FIN}&=B_t^{FIN}, \\
 L_t^{PROP}&=B_t^{RE}+B_t^{HOU}, \\
 L_t^{HHN}&=B_t^{HH}-B_t^{HOU}.
 \label{eq:primary_crosswalk}
\end{align}
The included stock is therefore
\begin{equation}
 L_t^P=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}L_t^k=B_t^{TOT}-B_t^{LG},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K=\{NFB,FIN,PROP,HHN\}.
 \label{eq:included_stock}
\end{equation}
The published NFB residual contains the separately disclosed overseas-linked
series and a small unresolved remainder. We retain both explicitly in the
coverage audit rather than relabelling them as domestic borrower observations.
The scale coordinate used at horizon \(h\) is
\begin{equation}
  g_{t,h}^P=\Delta_h\log L_t^P.
  \label{eq:mapped_scale}
\end{equation}
Thus scale and composition refer to the same lenders, loan instrument, and
borrower population.

For composition, first form each bucket's net change and positive part,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_t^k=L_t^k-L_{t-1}^k,
  \qquad
  P_t^k=\max\{\Delta L_t^k,0\},
  \qquad C_t=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal K}P_t^\ell.
  \label{eq:bucket_positive}
\end{equation}
For a window of \(w\) quarters, define
\begin{equation}
  P_{t,w}^k=\sum_{j=0}^{w-1}P_{t-j}^k,
  \qquad
  \pi_{t,w}^k
    =\frac{P_{t,w}^k}{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal K}P_{t,w}^{\ell}}.
  \label{eq:borrower_composition}
\end{equation}
The primary empirical object is the four-quarter vector
\(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{t,4}=(\pi_{t,4}^{NFB},\pi_{t,4}^{FIN},
\pi_{t,4}^{PROP},\pi_{t,4}^{HHN})\). The scalar
\(q_t\equiv\pi_{t,4}^{NFB}\) is a display coordinate. Four-quarter aggregation
reduces quarter-specific stock-difference noise and remains defined whenever
the four-quarter positive-flow denominator is positive. Neither the vector nor
\(q_t\) is a share of credit used for production, investment, or asset
purchases.

\subsection{Granularity invariance and claim boundary}

Suppose a component of \(L_t^k\) is divided into additive subseries
\(s_1,\ldots,s_m\) that remain in bucket \(k\). Then
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{m}\Delta L_{s_j,t}=\Delta L_{s,t}.
\]
Because the positive part in equation \eqref{eq:bucket_positive} is applied
after this sum, \(P_t^k\), \(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{t,w}\), and \(q_t\) are unchanged.
The measure is therefore invariant to classification refinements within a
fixed borrower bucket. It is not invariant to moving a sector across buckets.
That remaining classification uncertainty is addressed by three
literature-anchored mappings reported jointly rather than by presenting a
single author choice as uniquely implied by the data.

This construction differs from
\(\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_k}\max\{\Delta L_{s,t},0\}\). The latter rises when
offsetting expansions and contractions are published as separate subseries and
therefore depends on classification granularity. The empirical audit reports
the difference between the two orders rather than silently treating them as
equivalent.

\section{Data and Empirical Design}

\subsection{BOJ source, population, and timing}

The source is the BOJ Time-Series Data Search, database LA01. The selected map
is the total outstanding loans of domestically licensed banks less
local-government loans. The lender designation does not restrict borrowers to
Japan: the published NFB residual includes the disclosed overseas-linked
series. BOJ documentation states that the sector data have a discontinuity in
2009Q2 following an industrial-classification revision
\citep{BOJStatisticsNotes}. A change ending in 2009Q2 crosses that break, so
the first valid within-taxonomy change is 2009Q3. The first complete
four-quarter measure is dated 2010Q2 and becomes usable in the application in
2010Q3 under an assumed fixed 90-day release lag.

The long-term JGB yield, nominal GDP, and BOJ total assets are proxy outcomes
used only to demonstrate the bridge; none measures borrower composition or
validates it. Table~\ref{tab:destination_oos_incremental} reports the JGB
application, nominal GDP is not tabulated, and BOJ balance-sheet acceleration
is confined to the Appendix.

\subsection{Pre-outcome measurement audits}

\paragraph{Aggregation order and granularity.}
Signed changes are summed inside each fixed bucket before the positive part is
taken. Refinements within a bucket leave the result unchanged; moves across
buckets do not.

\paragraph{Positive and signed changes.}
The positive vector describes expansion. The signed vector retains
contractions and reconciles to the change in the included stock. Its ratios
may fall outside \([0,1]\), so they are not reported as composition shares.

\paragraph{Purpose and borrower-side comparators.}
The BOJ consumer-purpose household stock is compared with
\(L_t^{HHN}=B_t^{HH}-B_t^{HOU}\). It does not define the residual and is not an
input to \(q_t\). As a same-provider, narrower measure, it is only a
consistency check. MOF and MLIT data provide independent checks for selected
branches, not for the full four-bucket composition.

\subsubsection{Positive and net changes}

The positive coordinates sum to one, while signed changes reconcile to the
included stock change. The four-quarter vector is available for 64 of 67 valid
within-taxonomy changes, beginning in 2010Q2. The other endpoints are lost to
the rolling window and source availability.

\subsubsection{Literature-anchored mappings and limited validation}

The household non-housing residual is non-negative in every reported row and
its stock identity has zero maximum gap. Its correlation with the narrower BOJ
consumer-purpose stock is 0.904 over 67 paired observations, with a median
absolute stock gap of 35,947 in units of JPY 100 million. As both series come
from the BOJ and cover different concepts, this is a consistency check only.
Table~\ref{tab:external_partial_validation} adds selected independent checks.

\subsection{Use example: pseudo-OOS loss comparison}

For horizon \(h\in\{4,8\}\), the use example augments the matched BOJ credit-
stock-growth baseline with a reported taxonomy coordinate \(z_t^{(r)}\):
\begin{equation}
  y_{t,t+h}
    =\alpha_t+\beta_t g_{t,h}^P+\gamma_t z_t^{(r)}
     +\varepsilon_{t+h},
  \label{eq:oos_application}
\end{equation}
where \(r\) denotes the Bezemer--Samarina--Zhang NFB coordinate, the Werner
financial-circulation coordinate, or the M\"uller--Verner non-tradable
coordinate. Thus Table~\ref{tab:destination_oos_incremental} uses the bridge
without treating the three classifications as interchangeable.

The design uses current-vintage data and fixed release lags; historical
vintages are not reconstructed. Unavailable forward outcomes are purged, and
predictors are standardised within each expanding training window. The
moving-block bootstrap uses a block length equal to the horizon and 2,000
fixed-seed replications. Its interval covers the mean difference in
standardised squared-error loss, not the difference in RMSE.

The MOF comparison uses four-quarter changes in manufacturing corporations'

but their populations do not match the full BOJ measure

\subsection{Implications for data design}

A purpose measure would need gross originations classified by both borrower
and use, with repayments, write-offs, and refinancing reported separately.
Stable taxonomies, data vintages, and reconciliation to a published aggregate
would also be needed. Until then, borrower-composition releases should retain
their source mapping, positive and net versions, residual accounting, and
alternative classifications.

\section{Limits}

The measure has clear boundaries:
\begin{itemize}
  \item It observes the sector of the borrower, not the purpose, destination,
        recipient, collateral, or final expenditure financed by a loan.
  \item Positive stock changes are not gross originations. Repayment,
        refinancing, write-offs, and reclassification can change the stocks.
  \item Granularity invariance holds for refinements within a fixed bucket, not
        for moving a sector between reported mappings; construction makes
        this dependence visible.
  \item Residual NFB includes an overseas-linked component and a small
        unresolved component. The external sources cover only selected
        branches under different populations.
  \item Fixed release lags do not remove look-ahead from historical revisions,
        and the application sample is too short for predictive or causal
        inference.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}

The four-bucket crosswalk turns Japanese sectoral loan stocks into a
reproducible borrower-composition measure matched to credit scale. Published
alternatives show how the result changes when construction is classified
differently, while the accounting and coverage checks make the mapping
auditable.

The external checks remain partial, and the forecasting application shows no
reliable improvement over the matched baseline. The result is a measurement
bridge, not a measure of loan purpose or a forecasting result.
